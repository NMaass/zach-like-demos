# Design Notes

## What these slices are testing

The collection is not trying to prove that railroad shunting, bookbinding, or stage rigging can support a hundred-hour campaign. It asks an earlier and more important question:

> Does manipulating the system feel legible, expressive, precise, and pleasurable enough to justify deeper puzzle design?

The launcher intentionally gives all three prototypes the same shell, evaluation dimensions, progress model, and work-order framing. Differences in expert response should therefore come primarily from the construction system rather than presentation completeness.

## Shared interaction contract

Every workshop provides:

- a persistent workbench with one clear geometry owner;
- a small tool chest with numeric shortcuts;
- direct manipulation plus selection-based revision;
- undo, redo, reset, and deterministic inspection;
- stable metric tracks with tabular numbers;
- story presented as believable commissions rather than abstract level labels;
- local save state per work order;
- an expert notebook that does not interrupt play.

The interface follows a stable-workspace rule: a non-spatial action must not create incidental displacement. The top bar, work order, workbench, tool chest, and command bar are independently anchored. Status labels and test-button labels reserve their complete finite state space.

## Coldwater Junction

### Vocabulary

1. Straight rail
2. Quarter curve
3. Alternating spring turnout
4. Crossing
5. Lift rail

### Core promise

The player draws a yard rather than driving every car. On inspection, complete cuts are dispatched automatically. Turnouts alternate branches when entered from their stem, which turns a tiny spatial vocabulary into sequencing machinery without introducing a programming language.

### Puzzle progression

The ten orders move from a single connection to alternating sorts, constrained terrain, merge timing, crossings, two simultaneous cuts, and a four-destination final yard. All canonical solutions fit within the published rail budgets.

## Bellweather Bindery

### Vocabulary

1. Fold one side over another at a live crease
2. Trim an outside row through every current layer
3. Stitch a finished edge

### Core promise

A fold is not a visual toggle. Every leaf has a front, back, orientation, position, and stack order. The engine flips and reverses stacks as physical paper would. The approved dummy therefore specifies visible marks *and hidden structure*.

### Puzzle progression

The orders introduce asymmetric folds, perpendicular folds, binding edges, pre-fold trimming, multiple trims, and increasingly large signatures. The final work order collapses sixteen printed panels into one bound keepsake with four folds.

## The Orpheum Fly Loft

### Vocabulary

1. Fixed head block
2. Traveling block
3. Orthogonal rope segment
4. Counterweight bricks
5. Strike block or line

### Core promise

The player builds a real diagram whose graph and geometry matter. Fixed blocks redirect a rope. Traveling blocks double mechanical advantage and divide load travel. Inspection verifies connectivity, node degree, obstacle clearance, crossings, effort, travel, block count, and available iron.

### Puzzle progression

The orders begin with a one-to-one teaser, then introduce 2:1 and 4:1 systems, blocked sight lines, independent cues, counterweight constraints, and a three-line opening-night plot.

## Deliberate omissions

These slices do not include economies, campaign maps, procedural levels, user-generated puzzles, online leaderboards, or decorative meta-progression. Those systems would make the demonstrations look larger while making the central question harder to answer.
